Start here

Two lanes, and only one of them is durable

Since 22 July 2025 the Skilled Worker route generally requires a role skilled to RQF level 6 (graduate level). Plain “Data analyst” sits below that line. It still gets sponsored — but only through a temporary carve-out with a hard expiry date and a family cost. Which lane a vacancy falls into is decided by the occupation code the employer assigns, not by the job title on the advert, so this is the single highest-leverage thing to screen for.

Expiring lane RQF 3–5

Sponsored as “Data analyst”

3544Data analysts£34,900

Removed from general eligibility in July 2025 and kept alive only on the Temporary Shortage List. Roles coded here can still be sponsored today, at a lower salary bar than the general threshold.

The catch: shortage-list sponsorship at RQF 3–5 carries no right to bring dependants and no salary or visa-fee discounts — and every entry is set to lapse on 31 December 2026 unless the Migration Advisory Committee recommends an extension. A visa granted under it is fine; a renewal after expiry is not guaranteed.
Durable lane RQF 6+

Sponsored as a professional code

2433Actuaries, economists & statisticians£55,100
2133IT business analysts, architects & systems designers£54,900
2139IT professionals n.e.c.£52,300
2132IT managers£55,000

Full Skilled Worker eligibility, no expiry date, dependants permitted. The salary bar is materially higher — which is exactly why seniority helps you rather than hurting you here.

Why this favours you: a Lead / Senior / Principal analytics title, or one framed around BI architecture, statistics or analytics engineering, is far easier for an employer to code at RQF 6 than a plain analyst role. Aim one rung up. It widens the visa, not just the salary.

Before you apply

Four checks that save wasted applications

01

Confirm the licence, not the intention

An employer saying “we may consider sponsorship” means nothing without a licence. Search the company on the GOV.UK register of licensed sponsors — it is a free CSV updated at least monthly — or use the tier 1 checker. Confirm the route says Skilled Worker, not only Temporary Worker.

02

Check the salary clears the right bar

The floor is £41,700 or the occupation's going rate, whichever is higher — so a £38,000 “Data Analyst” advert cannot be sponsored under a professional code even by a licensed employer. Rule out anything materially below the bar for the code you are targeting before you spend time on it.

03

Ask which SOC code they would assign

The one question that resolves everything, and it is entirely reasonable to ask at first-stage screening. “If you were to sponsor this role, which SOC occupation code would it be assigned to?” A 2xxx answer is the durable lane. 3544 means the shortage list, no dependants, and an expiry you should discuss openly.

04

Raise it early, and frame it as solved

Sponsorship costs an employer a fee and some paperwork, not months of delay. Say plainly what you need and what it involves — silence until offer stage is what actually loses roles. If you are already in the UK on a Graduate or Student visa, lead with that: switching in-country is markedly simpler than sponsoring from overseas.
